## Detailed Entries

### 1. benefit (noun / verb)

**Pronunciation**: /ˈben.ə.fɪt/

**Definitions**:
1. *(noun)* A helpful or good effect, or something that improves a situation.
2. *(verb)* To be helped or improved by something; to receive an advantage.

**Word Family**:
- Noun: benefit / beneficiary
- Verb: benefit
- Adjective: beneficial
- Adverb: beneficially

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* Employees at the company receive health insurance as a benefit of full-time work.
2. *(Academic)* Researchers found that students benefit from taking short breaks during long study sessions.
3. *(Everyday)* Walking to work every day has many benefits, like better health and lower costs.

**Common Collocations**: a major benefit, benefit from (something), for the benefit of, mutual benefit, health benefits

**Learning Tip**: Think of *bene-* as in "benevolent" (good) + "fit" — something that is "good for" you. If it's a *benefit*, it's something *good* that *fits* your needs.

### 2. demonstrate (verb)

**Pronunciation**: /ˈdem.ən.streɪt/

**Definitions**:
1. To show clearly that something is true by giving proof, facts, or examples.
2. To show someone how to do something or how a thing works.

**Word Family**:
- Noun: demonstration / demonstrator
- Verb: demonstrate
- Adjective: demonstrative
- Adverb: demonstratively

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* The sales assistant demonstrated how the new printer works before we bought it.
2. *(Academic)* The experiment was designed to demonstrate that plants grow faster in sunlight.
3. *(Everyday)* My sister demonstrated the dance steps slowly so I could follow along.

**Common Collocations**: demonstrate how to, demonstrate that, clearly demonstrate, demonstrate a skill, give a demonstration

**Learning Tip**: Break it down: "de-" + "monstrate" (related to "monster," which once meant "to show" or "a warning sign"). To *demonstrate* is to *show* something openly, like pointing at it so everyone can see.

### 3. considerable (adjective)

**Pronunciation**: /kənˈsɪd.ər.ə.bəl/

**Definitions**:
1. Large in amount, size, or degree; big enough to be noticed or to matter.
2. Deserving attention or respect because of its size or importance.

**Word Family**:
- Noun: consideration
- Verb: consider
- Adjective: considerable
- Adverb: considerably

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* The new marketing plan required a considerable amount of time and money.
2. *(Academic)* There was a considerable difference between the two test results.
3. *(Everyday)* We spent a considerable amount of time looking for a parking spot downtown.

**Common Collocations**: considerable amount, considerable time, considerable effort, considerable difference, a considerable number of

**Learning Tip**: "Considerable" sounds like "consider" + "able." If something is big enough that you have to *consider* it (think about it carefully), it's *considerable*.
