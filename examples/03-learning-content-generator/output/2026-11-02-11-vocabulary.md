## Detailed Entries

### 1. benefit (noun)

**Pronunciation**: /ˈbɛn.ɪ.fɪt/

**Definitions**:
1. An advantage, profit, or helpful effect gained from something.
2. (often plural) Money, services, or other help provided by an employer, government, or insurance policy (e.g., "health benefits").

**Word Family**:
- Noun: benefit
- Verb: to benefit (from)
- Adjective: beneficial
- Adverb: beneficially

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* Employees at this company receive health insurance as a benefit of full-time employment.
2. *(Academic)* Researchers found that regular exercise has a significant benefit for mental health.
3. *(Everyday)* Walking to work instead of driving has the benefit of saving money on gas.

**Common Collocations**: benefit from, mutual benefit, health benefit, give someone the benefit of the doubt

**Learning Tip**: Split the word into "bene" (Latin for "good") + "fit." A benefit is something that is literally "good for" you — just like "benefactor" means someone who does good for others.

### 2. opportunity (noun)

**Pronunciation**: /ˌɑː.pɚˈtuː.nə.ti/

**Definitions**:
1. A set of circumstances that makes it possible to do something; a chance to achieve or do something.
2. A favorable position or opening, especially in a career or business context.

**Word Family**:
- Noun: opportunity
- Verb: (no direct form; use "take the opportunity")
- Adjective: opportune / opportunistic
- Adverb: opportunely

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* The new project gives junior staff an opportunity to work directly with senior managers.
2. *(Academic)* Studying abroad offers students the opportunity to experience a different culture firsthand.
3. *(Everyday)* I didn't want to miss the opportunity to see my old friend before she moved away.

**Common Collocations**: golden opportunity, job opportunity, take the opportunity, miss an opportunity

**Learning Tip**: Think "open door" — the beginning sound "op-" is like "open." An opportunity is an open door leading to something good.

2. To attain a particular, usually desirable, result or standard.

**Word Family**:
- Noun: achievement
- Verb: achieve
- Adjective: achievable
- Adverb: (not commonly used)

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* Our sales team worked overtime to achieve the quarterly target.
2. *(Academic)* To achieve a high score on the exam, students must review all the course material.
3. *(Everyday)* She finally achieved her goal of running a full marathon.

**Common Collocations**: achieve a goal, achieve success, achieve results, achieve a balance

**Learning Tip**: Hear "a-CHIEVE" as becoming the "chief" of your goal — when you achieve something, you rise to the top, like a chief.

### 5. directly (adverb)

**Pronunciation**: /dɪˈrɛkt.li/

**Definitions**:
1. In a straight line or course; without any detour or delay.
2. Without anyone or anything intervening; immediately.

**Word Family**:
- Noun: direction
- Verb: direct
- Adjective: direct
- Adverb: directly

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* Please contact the manager directly if you have any questions about the schedule.
2. *(Academic)* The results are directly related to the amount of sunlight each plant received.
3. *(Everyday)* After the movie, we went directly home because it was late.

**Common Collocations**: directly related to, speak directly, directly involved, directly opposite

**Learning Tip**: "Directly" = "direct" + "-ly." If you go "direct," you go straight with no stops — "directly" describes doing something in that same straight, immediate way.
