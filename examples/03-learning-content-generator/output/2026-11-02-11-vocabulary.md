## Detailed Entries

### 1. purpose (noun)

**Pronunciation**: /ˈpɜː.pəs/ (UK) · /ˈpɝː.pəs/ (US)

**Definitions**:
1. The reason for which something is done or created; the aim behind an action.
2. A need or use that something serves (e.g., "for business purposes").

**Word Family**:
- Noun: purpose
- Verb: (no common verb form; use "intend" or "aim")
- Adjective: purposeful
- Adverb: purposefully

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* The purpose of today's meeting is to review the quarterly budget.
2. *(Academic)* The purpose of this study is to examine how sleep affects memory.
3. *(Everyday)* She bought a notebook for the purpose of keeping a daily journal.

**Common Collocations**: the purpose of, serve a purpose, on purpose, sense of purpose

**Learning Tip**: Think of "purpose" as your "pur-POSE" — the position or stance you take on *why* you're doing something. If someone asks "What's the point?", "purpose" is the answer.

### 2. result (noun)

**Pronunciation**: /rɪˈzʌlt/

**Definitions**:
1. Something that happens or exists because of something else that happened before it; an outcome.
2. (academic) A score or finding produced by a test, experiment, or calculation.

**Word Family**:
- Noun: result
- Verb: result (in)
- Adjective: resulting
- Adverb: (not commonly used)

**Example Sentences**:
1. *(Professional)* As a result of the new schedule, productivity increased by 20 percent.
2. *(Academic)* The experiment's results showed a clear link between diet and energy levels.
3. *(Everyday)* He studied hard all semester, and the result was his best grade yet.

**Common Collocations**: as a result (of), get a result, end result, result in

**Learning Tip**: Picture "re-SULT" as something that "sprang" (like "sault" in French, "to jump") out from a cause — a result is what jumps out at the end of a process.
